GC SERVER JUDICIAL COMMITTEE

Official Judgment: Rejection of Appeal & Invocation of Article V

Appellant: Kareem (عضو كريم)[cite: 3]

Subject: Appeal against disciplinary suspension of soundboard permissions and clip submissions[cite: 3]

Governing Document: The GC Constitution & Server Rules[cite: 1, 2]

Ruling Status: UNFOUNDED & REJECTED IN FULL

The GC Judiciary and Administration have thoroughly reviewed the appeal memorandum submitted by Kareem. After meticulous examination of the arguments, constitutional articles, and established factual evidence, the court has determined that the appeal lacks merit, distorts the interpretation of the GC Constitution, and relies on self-contradictory claims. Consequently, the appeal is officially REJECTED.

Detailed Judicial Rebuttal to Appellant's Claims

1. Rebuttal to Procedural Defense: The Right to Appeal & Article V

Appellant's Claim: Kareem claims that his right to appeal is protected under Article IV, and that the notice's statement declaring appeals closed was null and void.

The Court's Ruling: While Article IV guarantees the right to challenge a penalty[cite: 2], the appellant conveniently ignores Article V (Unfounded Challenges), which explicitly mandates: "If the judges determine that a challenge to a penalty is unfounded, an additional penalty shall be imposed on the member who made the challenge."[cite: 2] The original notice correctly assessed the overwhelming evidence of guilt. By submitting this baseless appeal, Kareem has officially triggered Article V.

2. Rebuttal to Non-Retroactivity & Timing Claims

Appellant's Claim: Kareem argues that the action occurred before the GC Constitution was enacted, invoking the principle of non-retroactivity.

The Court's Ruling: The core tenets of the GC community—fairness, trust, and the prohibition of manipulating system structures for personal or associate advantage—have always been foundational. Furthermore, Article I strictly codifies the protection of soundboards and establishes immediate permanent prohibitions for unauthorized deletions, aligning directly with the established facts of this case[cite: 1, 2].

3. Rebuttal to Investigation & Legal Representation Claims (Article II)

Appellant's Claim: Kareem alleges a lack of investigation and a denial of his right to a lawyer under Article II[cite: 3].

The Court's Ruling: Article II grants members the right to appoint a lawyer, but explicitly notes that "Appointing a lawyer does not automatically suspend or cancel a penalty."[cite: 2] Furthermore, under the foundational Principle of Factual Judgment, decisions are made "based solely on what actually occurred, the established facts, and the available evidence." When a violation is caught red-handed through undeniable system manipulation, formal procedural hearings do not override clear physical evidence[cite: 2].

4. Rebuttal to Intent & The Admission of Guilt Regarding the Soundboard

Appellant's Claim: Kareem argues he had no harmful intent, that the deleted soundboard was his own voice ("زوقو"), and that he deleted it merely to free up slots for general use[cite: 3].

The Court's Ruling: Kareem’s own defense constitutes a full admission of guilt under Article I. He openly admits he deleted his own personalized soundboard containing his voice[cite: 3]. Article I explicitly states:
"Special protection applies to soundboards created for a specific member or containing their voice, words, or personalized content... If the deleted soundboard was created by the offender or specifically belongs to them, a personal soundboard associated with the offender shall also be added as an additional penalty alongside the initial punishment."[cite: 2]
His excuse of "freeing slots" is completely invalidated by the established evidence proving he manipulated permissions to curate space for soundboards benefiting himself and his associates[cite: 1].

5. Rebuttal to Penalty Overreach (Clip Ban)

Appellant's Claim: Kareem claims the penalty overstepped by banning clip submissions, arguing the Constitution only mentions soundboards[cite: 3].

The Court's Ruling: When a member engages in administrative sabotage and exploits community privileges for personal advantage, the administration and judges retain sovereign authority to suspend related community privileges (including clip submissions) to protect server integrity. Moreover, because this appeal has been proven entirely unfounded, his request to lift the clip ban is denied[cite: 1, 3].

FINAL JUDICIAL DECREE

1. The appeal submitted by Kareem is hereby REJECTED in its entirety due to lack of merit and direct contradiction with established evidence and constitutional articles[cite: 1, 2, 3].

2. The original suspension of soundboard permissions and rejection of clip submissions is UPHELD PERMANENTLY[cite: 1].

3. Pursuant to Article V (Unfounded Challenges), an additional penalty is formally invoked and added to Kareem's disciplinary record for submitting a baseless challenge[cite: 2].
